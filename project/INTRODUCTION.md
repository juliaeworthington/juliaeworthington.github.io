## What's going on with Team Vitality?
Team Vitality dominated the competitive Counter-Strike 2 scene for most of 2025 and the first third of 2026. The French team stood at the #1 spot on both of the Counter-Strike ranking charts, HLTV and VRS ([source](https://www.hltv.org/team/9565/vitality)). They obliterated every team in their path, boasting a 90.1% map win rate, while not dropping a single match between January 31 and April 14 of 2026 ([source](https://liquipedia.net/counterstrike/List_of_Longest_LAN_winning_streaks/Vitality_2026)). After winning two of the largest events in 2025, the BLAST Austin Major 2025 and the StarLadder Budapest Major 2025, they won first place at every event they attended in 2026, becoming the first team to win two ESL Grandslams after IEM Rio 2026. This was the first time a team had shown such control of the competitive scene since the Astralis "era" of 2018 and 2019. The Danish team, which ranked #1 on HLTV for 13 consecutive months, is widely regarded as the greatest team to ever compete in the game's predecessor, Counter-Strike: Global Offensive ([source](https://www.hltv.org/news/33042/gone-but-not-to-be-forgotten-the-legacy-of-astralis-the-greatest-team-of-all-time-part-1)). The comparisons to Astralis were numerous, among fans and professional analysts alike. Was this a new "era" of competitive Counter-Strike? But just as quickly as the "era" discussion began, it came to an end. During IEM Atlanta in mid-May, things took a turn for the worse. This event marked the end not only of Vitality's 5-event consecutive 1st-place streak, but also of a 21-event consecutive top-4 finish streak, as well as a 15-map consecutive win streak against Natus Vincere, previously an easy opponent, who eliminated Vitality from the event ([source](https://esports.gg/news/counter-strike-2/iem-atlanta-2026-details-results/)). For the next four months, Vitality did not reach the finals at any of the events they attended, until StarLadder StarSeries Fall 2026. Currently at the #5 spot in both HLTV and VRS standings, their rankings have slowly fallen over the past five months. This has left many Counter-Strike fans wondering: is this the end of the Vitality era?

Counter-Strike 2 is a first-person shooter game that consists of gameplay on two opposing sides: terrorists (T-side) and counter-terrorists (CT-side). The objectives are simple: the terrorists plant and defend a C4 explosive on either the A or B bombsite; the counter-terrorists prevent them from doing so, defusing the bomb if necessary. Maps are chosen by teams alternating between picking maps they win and banning maps they don't. After a map is chosen for play, teams play 12 rounds on their starting side before swapping for the remaining 12. The first team to 13 rounds wins the map; if both teams get to 12, the map goes into overtime. Matches are played in best of one, best of three, and best of five formats, with best of three being the most common and best of five typically being reserved for event grand finals. While the game's premise is simple, pro-gameplay is not. Throughout a match, teams must use strategy and manage their economy, with each player in a team of five playing or alternating between roles: rifler, AWPer (sniper), IGL (in-game leader), anchor, lurker, entry, or support. Each role is a different job: riflers and AWPers get kills, IGLs are the team captains; anchors defend bomb sites pre-plant (CT-side only); lurkers "lurk" to get kills by catching their enemies off-guard late in the round; entry players go out first to get as many kills as possible; support players throw utility (grenades, flashbangs, smokes, etc.) ([source](https://esportsinsider.com/counter-strike-roles-explained)). Team Vitality consists of Dan "apEX" Madesclaire (IGL/support), Robin "ropz" Kool (lurker/anchor), Mathieu "ZywOo" Herbaut (AWPer), Shahar "flameZ" Shushan (rifler/entry), and William "mezii" Merriman (anchor/rifler). A player's K/D (kills to deaths) ratio and ADR (average damage per round) vary depending on their role; AWPers tend to do more damage and get more kills (meaning ZywOo is usually at the top of the scoreboard), while IGLs tend to do the least damage and get the fewest kills (meaning apEX is usually at the bottom of the scoreboard). But team performance is more than just K/D and ADR. A team's performance depends on each player's ability to perform their role as part of a larger puzzle, with each round's strategy widely varying between scenarios (pre- vs post- bomb plant, T vs CT side, good vs bad economy). The game's complexity makes Team Vitality's downfall since IEM Atlanta difficult to analyze. While fans call for the team to make roster changes, it is unclear what, or who, is the real problem.


## Research Questions
1. Is a decline in player performance to blame for Vitality's recent trophy drought? If so, which players could be the problem, and why is their performance weaker than before?
2. Was there ever a Vitality era? Does their performance during their peak compare to that of Astralis during their era?
3. Is this the end of an era? How does the current state of Vitality compare to that of Astralis during their downfall? 
4. Overpass, a game map, was removed on July 6, 2026, and replaced with a different map, Cache. Has the removal of Overpass impacted Vitality's performance during recent events?
5. Was Vitality's loss to Natus Vincere inevitable? Why did Vitality lose a team they had previously been so strong against?
6. Team Vitality took time off between IEM Rio in mid-April, BLAST Rivals in late April, and IEM Atlanta in mid-May, opting to vacation in the Americas instead of returning to Europe to practice. How did this impact their performance at the latter two events?
7. Vitality's dry streak ended on September 20, 2026, when they won 1st place at StarLadder StarSeries Fall 2026. What makes this event different from IEM Atlanta and the four events that followed it?
8. At that same event, Vitality was knocked to the lower bracket by Aurora, whom they later defeated in the grand final. 
9. Vitality is well-known for doing research into their opponents before matches. Does Vitality struggle against teams that have made recent roster changes or are using stand-ins?
10. At BLAST Open Porto 2026, Justinas "jL" Lekavicius played as a stand-in for mezii, who was expecting a child. How did this affect the team's performance at this event?
