Team Vitality was in total domination of the competitive Counter-Strike 2 scene for most of 2025 and the first third of 2026. The French team had won first place at every event they attended in 2026, absolutely obliterating every team in their path. They stood at the #1 spot on both of the well-respected Counter-Strike charts, HLTV and VRS. This was the first time a team had shown such control of the competitive scene since Astralis, a Danish team, who ranked #1 on HLTV for 13 consecutive months in 2018-2019, playing the game's predecessor, Counter-Strike: Global Offensive. The comparisons to Astralis were numerous, among fans and professional analysts alike. Was this a new "era" of competitive Counter-Strike? That was until IEM Atlanta in mid-May, when things took a turn for the worse. This event marked the end, not just of Vitality's 5-event consecutive 1st-place streak, but also of a 21-event-long consecutive top-4 finish streak, as well as a 15-map consecutive win streak against their biggest competition, Natus Vincere, who eliminated Vitality from the event. Since then, Vitality has not reached the finals at any of the events they've attended. This has left many Counter-Strike fans wondering: is this the end of an era? 




Research Questions: Why is Vitality's performance worse than it was during the first half of the year? Many people are comparing Vitality to Astralis, who famously dominated the Counter-Strike scene from 2018 to 2019 before their downfall. How does the current state of Vitality compare to that of Astralis during their downfall? Overpass, a map frequently played by Vitality, was removed from the map pool in early July of this year. Has the removal of Overpass impacted Vitality's performance during recent events?
